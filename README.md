# playwood. APP

A local, full-window furniture design workspace built with Vite, React, TypeScript, Three.js, React Three Fiber, and shadcn/ui components backed by Radix. Warm neutral colors, restrained controls, and independent scrolling inspectors keep the assembly in view.

For implementation details, see the [architecture and development documentation](docs/README.md). Coding agents should start with [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Run

Requires Node.js 22.12+ (tested with Node 25).

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. No accounts, API keys, backend, or external fonts are required. Projects autosave in this browser's local storage; export a JSON project to keep a portable copy.

## Design workflow

- Start with a shelf, desk, display, a resource example from the Library, or a blank project. **New project** in the header opens a confirmation before clearing the workspace; choosing a full build from the Library or Templates also asks before replacing it. Use **Export current project** there to keep a separate copy, then **Start new project** or **Replace project**. The browser save is replaced too; Undo can restore the previous project until you reload or close the app.
- Add your own panels with dimensions in millimeters, material, finish color, and thickness. Choose solid, pegboard, or rounded slots; holes are real openings through the mesh.
- Panel finish colors come from the project palette. Use **Add color** to create a named swatch, or **Manage palette** to rename, edit, or remove custom colors. The color picker edits a local draft; **Save color** commits it once, then click its swatch to apply it. Editing a palette color updates its uses throughout the project; removing an in-use color lets you choose a replacement. Palette changes support undo/redo and are saved automatically and included in project exports. Older projects' unlisted panel colors become named swatches on opening. Connectors keep their available stock colors.
- **Finish coverage**, under **Material & finish**, blends from **0%** natural material grain to **100%** solid finish color. Intermediate values show tinted grain. Matching selections can change together; a **Mixed** coverage field is disabled. Coverage changes support undo/redo, autosave, and project files. Assembly instances have their own coverage override under **Appearance**, separate from panel color.
- Open **Shape & surface** in the panel inspector and choose **Create custom outline** to open the vector editor. Drag points, double-click an edge (or use **Add point**) to insert a vertex, or enter exact X/Y coordinates in millimeters from the top left. Grid snapping, Shift to lock a drag axis, Alt to bypass snapping, arrow-key nudging, zoom/pan, and local undo/redo support precise edits. **Apply outline** saves one project edit; **Cancel** discards the draft. Outlines scale with panel width and height, and corner radius rounds both outward and inward corners. The inspector shows the finished shape; **Reset to rectangle** removes the custom outline. Project files retain the normalized vertices and cutting lists export their millimeter coordinates. Connector placement and overlap checks still use the panel’s rectangular bounds; automatic rectangular splitting is unavailable for custom outlines.
- Select a solid horizontal rectangular panel and open **TROFAST inserts → Add TROFAST item** to fit an IKEA box or compartment tray through a cutout. Choose from eight plastic, mesh, and tray shapes and their available IKEA Switzerland colors. The rim rests on the panel and the body hangs below. The editor checks footprint, a 20 mm border, the floor, surrounding panels (including hidden ones), other inserts, and connector clearance. Rotate the item, click or drag the panel diagram, use its arrow keys (10 mm; Shift: 1 mm), or enter exact center coordinates measured from the panel’s top-left corner. A live 3D preview remains separate until **Add TROFAST box** or **Save TROFAST changes**; Cancel/Escape discards it. Add multiple items where space permits, and edit or remove them from the same section. Items and openings follow their panel, support undo/redo and autosave, and survive project/assembly exports. Product sizes stay fixed when panels resize. The current catalogue is based on IKEA Switzerland; colors and rim/cutout profiles are visual approximations. **Measure the real item before cutting:** the displayed opening and exported cutout notes are not fabrication templates.
- Select a panel in the model or panel list. **Dimensions** and **Connection** or **Placement** come first in the inspector. Expand **Shape & surface** or **Material & finish** for secondary properties; section expansion is remembered for the session. Open **Build tools** operations only when needed. Change dimensions, position, and orientation in their sections. Width and height edits apply on Enter or when leaving the field; connected panels adjust together to keep their joints aligned.
- Open **Snap** in the preview to independently enable the **global grid (G)** and **geometry alignment (S)**. Set grid spacing and snap tolerance in millimeters; preferences are remembered in this browser. The grid starts at the world origin. Geometry alignment uses world-axis panel boundaries (bounding extents for rotated panels), or other connector centers when moving connectors. **Show grid** controls guide visibility separately. Snapping applies to panel drags, resize arrows, and keyboard movement; with the grid enabled, arrows move one grid spacing and Shift moves ten. Joint and hardware limits still apply, and existing drag-to-attach behavior remains available.
- Select one panel or connector to show **resize arrows** in the preview. Drag an arrow to pull or push along a permitted direction, making the structure wider, taller, or deeper while keeping connector angles and panel thickness fixed. Panel edge handles keep the opposite edge in place; connected panels adapt together. Hold **Shift before dragging a panel edge arrow** to resize only that panel, keeping other panels and connectors in place. This stops where a joint, connector, or size limit prevents further resizing; arrows perpendicular to the panel cannot resize it independently. The preview shows the change in millimeters and stops at the valid size limit. Release to apply one undoable change, or press **Escape** to cancel. Resize handles are hidden during attachment, exploded view, multi-selection, and linked assembly editing.
- Select a vertical rectangular panel and use **Extend to ground** under **Dimensions** or in its context menu. The lower edge extends exactly to ground level (Y = 0); the upper edge, other panels, and existing connectors stay fixed. Rotated panels can extend along either width or height. The action explains when a joint, collision, assembly constraint, or size limit prevents the extension, and supports undo, redo, and autosave.
- Click a panel in the Studio or Realistic preview and use **arrow keys** to move it **1 cm**, or **Shift + arrow keys** for **10 cm**. Movement follows the current view and the panel’s rotated axes, including after orbiting behind or above the assembly. Connected panels resize while connector angles, thicknesses, and hardware sizes stay fixed. A move that cannot preserve the joints or panel limits leaves the project unchanged and explains why. Free panels and fully selected assemblies translate together. Holding a key forms one undo step. Arrow shortcuts are active only while the canvas has focus; fields and panel-list controls retain their own keys. Finish an attachment or close exploded view before moving panels.
- Press **X**, **Y**, or **Z** to rotate a selected panel or complete assembly by **90°** around that world axis through the selection’s center. Multiple selected objects turn together. Select all physically connected panels or detach them first; a partial connected selection stays unchanged and explains why. Each press is one undo step, with autosave and redo support. Rotation shortcuts pause during text editing, dialogs, attachment previews, dragging, assembly source editing, and exploded view.
- Drag a panel thumbnail to reorder the list. Hover or focus a row for rename, visibility, and remove controls; double-click a name to rename it in place. Amber markers identify overlaps and name the other panels on hover or focus. These changes autosave and support undo.
- Organize the **Panels** and **Connectors** tabs with one level of named groups. Use **New group**, or select rows and choose **Group selected** from the list controls or context menu. Drag a panel thumbnail or connector grip into a group, between groups, or onto **Ungrouped**; dragging a selected row moves the selection together. Use the group checkbox to select or deselect all its items, including while collapsed; a dash indicates a partial selection. Other selections in the same tab are preserved. Groups collapse, accept drops while collapsed, and rename with the pencil, double-click, or **F2**. The group context menu’s **Ungroup** action keeps its contents. Groups and list ordering autosave, export with the project, and support undo/redo.
- Shift-click, Ctrl/⌘-click, or use the panel checkboxes to select multiple panels. Shared parameters stay editable and apply to the entire selection; differing values show **Mixed** and are disabled. Related edits preserve mixed finishes and perforation spacing. Select all with **⌘/Ctrl A**.
- Press **B**, or choose **Box select** beside Pan view, then drag a rectangle around parts. Candidate parts highlight while drawing; release to select panels and connectors together and return to normal navigation. Hold **Shift** while drawing to add both kinds to the existing selection, or press **Escape** to cancel without changing it. Parts must be fully enclosed; the box reaches through overlapping parts and skips hidden parts. With a mixed selection, the inspector follows the active **Panels** or **Connectors** tab under Assembly. Selecting only one kind opens its inspector. Linked assemblies select as whole instances; while editing an assembly, the box selects only its own parts.
- Choose **Attach a panel** to connect a new or existing panel to any of six sides. Match the **A–F labels** in the inspector and preview, hover or focus either control to highlight its side, or click a preview label to select it. Side names stay attached to the panel as you orbit, and leader lines keep opposite sides identifiable in straight-on views. Explore 90°, 105°, 150°, T, and cross joints, offsets, insets, flip direction, connector color, and quantity. Ghost previews update as settings change.
- T and cross joints add the complete set of branches. Changes to a parent propagate to its connected descendants. Detaching preserves world positions.
- **Auto-add connectors**, under **Build tools** or in the toolbar, also upgrades existing corner or T joints into complete T or cross joints. Select the two panels to join; an unselected upright at the same seam can complete the joint. Clamps fit the straight overlap shared by every panel, including different panel sizes and offsets, and find room away from existing corner hardware. Matching panels keep their positions. If an edge needs a small size correction (up to 50 mm), **Resize panel to connect?** shows the proposed width or height before anything changes. **Resize and connect** adjusts that edge and adds the connectors together; **Cancel** keeps the project unchanged. With one panel selected, only that panel is offered for resizing. If an upgrade changes a reusable assembly's own hardware, that instance becomes independent; its source and other copies stay unchanged. The result reports upgraded joints and affected instances, and the entire operation supports Undo.
- **Connect with a panel** reverses this workflow: select two or more connectors in the preview or connector list, then use the inspector or context menu. Every piece needs a compatible free T/cross opening (for example, change selected 90° hardware to T first). The panel fits the actual connector positions, including slides and rotated designs. Opposing openings set its span; an unbounded end extends 360 mm, and the other edges cover the full 40 mm grips. Misalignment, occupied slots, incompatible stock, panel collisions, and obstructing hardware prevent the edit. Existing panels and hardware stay in place; affected assembly instances become independent. Supports undo, redo, autosave, and JSON round trips.
- Select two matching parallel rectangular panels and open **Build tools → Divide evenly** to choose the number of dividers. Only counts that fit the current joints and available space are offered; each divider adds one equal section. **Split in the middle** is the one-divider shortcut. Select all four matching sides of an opening for **Divide into a grid**, then set **X dividers** for columns and **Y dividers** for rows: one on each axis creates a 2 × 2 grid. Either axis can be zero, with at least one divider overall. Splitting preserves the outside dimensions, includes T and cross hardware as needed, and updates the cutting list in one undoable edit. It requires 18–19 mm rectangular stock and panels outside linked assemblies.
- The Everyday shelf uses eight 90° connectors at its corners and four T connectors at the middle shelf. Each side has separate lower and upper panels with the 35 mm gap required by the T body. Recognized copies of the former five-panel template are repaired on load/import while preserving finishes, names, shelf positions, and overall dimensions; the load repair supports Undo.
- Thickness, shared-edge, and embedded edge-joint errors block new attachments. Panel overlaps, inaccessible face clamp positions, and supports that have moved out of alignment appear as review notes. The inspector and parts list identify the actual connector types.
- Use **Rescale creation** above the canvas to change repeated measurements throughout the project. Matching widths and heights share one control; thickness, rounded corners, and active hole sizes and spacing have their own controls. The dialog shows use counts and allowed ranges, including connector-compatible thicknesses. Connected spans and fitted inserts adapt together while hardware stays its original size. All changes apply at once, preserve reusable assembly sources and instance controls, and support undo/redo and autosave. Conflicting values leave the project unchanged with an explanation.
- Use **Move creation** above the canvas to translate every panel and connector together, including hidden panels and separate pieces. Enter X, Y, and Z offsets in millimeters, or use **Center horizontally**, **Near back wall**, and **Place on floor** to set them. Negative X moves left; negative Z moves toward the back wall. The wall preset leaves 50 mm clearance. Click **Move creation** to apply the offsets without changing sizes, relative positions, or connections; Undo restores the previous placement.
- Use **Rotate creation** above the canvas to rotate the whole assembly by ±90° or ±45°. Choose Z to tilt sideways into a diagonal layout, X to tip forward/backward, or Y to turn left/right. Every panel (including hidden panels and separate pieces) and connector moves together around the assembly center; its lowest panel height stays unchanged. Rotation autosaves, supports undo/redo, and is preserved in exported projects and the room preview.
- Orbit, pan, zoom, inspect orthographic views, show dimensions, and explore the exploded view. Export editable JSON or a CSV cutting/parts list.
- Choose **Realistic preview** to see your assembled furniture at its actual scale in a modern room with daylight, oak flooring, and familiar furnishings. The 165 cm floor lamp provides a size reference; dimensions describe the visible assembly. Orbit and zoom to explore, press **F** to reset the room view, Click a panel and use arrow keys to adjust it here, or return to **Studio** (or press **Escape**) for the full inspector with your selection and view settings preserved. The room renders locally without external assets or API keys.
- Enable **Auto furnish** in the room card to add books and decor to shelves, a lamp and computer with keyboard and mouse to suitable tables, and seated people on benches or chairs at desks. It starts off; surface detection and extra rendering run only while enabled in Realistic preview. Placement uses level surfaces, dimensions, support, and available clearance, so ambiguous, tilted, or custom-outline surfaces may stay empty. Furnishings adapt to visible geometry, use the room's lighting, and never become project parts or change exports. The choice lasts for the current session; disable it to remove the added objects and reduce rendering work.
- Shelf compartments mix books, plants or vases, and intentionally empty space. Exposed cabinet tops stay clear or receive occasional decor. Books vary in count, color, thickness, height, and cover design, with standing rows supported at a side wall and leaning books at the free end. Placement uses each row's actual width and stays on the shelf even where connectors leave a gap to the wall. Boards without side support and short compartments can receive flat stacks. Arrangements stay stable while navigating the preview, and spines face the opening inferred from side and back panels.
- Bench detection also recognizes seats extending from shelving, with a leg at the free end and support through the shelving at the attached end. It uses the seat's long axis and tries clear positions along both long edges, so a shelf above one end does not exclude an exposed seating area. Seated figures need room for their body, head, and feet; narrow ledges, unsupported extensions, and fully obstructed seats stay unoccupied.

Keyboard: **B then drag** selects a rectangular area (**Shift** while drawing adds); **Arrows** move the preview selection 1 cm; **Shift + arrows** move 10 cm; **X / Y / Z** rotate the selection 90°; **F** fits the view; **⌘/Ctrl Z** undoes; **⌘/Ctrl Shift Z** redoes; **⌘/Ctrl D** duplicates; **Delete** removes a panel; **Escape** cancels selection/preview. Text fields retain normal editing shortcuts. The keyboard button beside the view controls opens the navigation and selection shortcut reference.

In the panel and connector lists, **F2** edits a focused panel or group name. Focus a thumbnail or connector grip and press **Space** to pick it up, **↑/↓** (or **Home/End**) to choose its position or group, then **Space/Enter** to drop or **Escape** to cancel. Renaming accepts **Enter** to save and **Escape** to cancel.

## Illustrated building guide

Choose **Guide** in the header (the book icon on small screens), or **Shortcuts & help** in the status bar, to open the field guide. Its 33 searchable techniques cover starting and replacing projects, panels, finishes and palettes, attachment and connector types, automatic joints, closing openings, equal and grid splitting, connected and independent panel resizing, movement and snapping, moving and rescaling the whole creation, rotation, copy/paste, box selection and groups, reusable assemblies and their editable controls, room scale and automatic furnishing, review, and project and assembly files.

Every technique has four steps with an illustrated example, exact controls, an explanation, the expected result, and relevant tips. Start with the suggested setup, move through **Next step**, or jump to a numbered step. Related guides connect techniques into longer workflows. The guide remembers your reading position for the current browser session; browsing it never changes the project. **Keyboard shortcuts** remains available as a separate quick reference.

Illustrations project real example panel geometry and connector models into SVG. Only the current step is drawn; help loads on demand and adds no WebGL viewport or continuous rendering loop. Smaller screens use a collapsible technique browser and a stacked lesson layout.

## Reusable assemblies

Build a box once and reuse its construction as linked instances. Select the box's panels, open **Build tools → Create assembly**, enter an **Assembly name**, and choose **Create assembly**. The selected panels become its first instance, and the reusable design appears under **My assemblies** in the Library. The **Cube** library example is a four-panel starting point.

Select an assembly or the panels around a rectangular opening, then open **Close holes** under **Build tools** in the inspector. One opening offers a direct action; multiple openings offer a choice such as Front or Back, with panel dimensions. Compartments are detected separately, including on rotated designs. Closing sizes and positions a solid panel for the connector slots and fits hardware along all four edges. Occupied joints are replaced with complete T or cross connectors where needed; an opening that cannot accept compatible hardware is left unchanged. For assemblies, the selected instance receives an independent design containing the new panel; other copies retain their openings. The closing panel participates in the connected geometry and follows compatible assembly size changes. Closing supports undo, redo, autosave, and export.

- Open **Editable values** when creating an assembly, or choose **Edit values** in **Edit shared design**, to add named controls. Choose the panel dimensions each value controls; one value can drive several panels and dimensions. For example, bind both dimensions of every cube panel to **Side length**. The first dimension sets the default, and further dimensions retain any size difference as an offset. **Relationships** lets you change each binding's multiplier and offset. Use **Save values** to apply shared control edits.
- The **Cube module** comes with one **Side length** control. **Wide shelf module**, tall boxes, and shallow boxes expose **Width**, **Height**, and **Depth** separately. Cubbies expose each column's width and each row's height, plus shared depth; columns run left to right and rows bottom to top. These values describe panel cut lengths in millimeters. Connector clearances and panel thickness add to the final outside dimensions.
- Configure a selected instance under **Dimensions** in its inspector. Its dimensions change without changing the source defaults or other instances' configured values. Connected panels and connectors adjust together, and linked neighbors move as needed to keep shared corners aligned. Incompatible shared depths, closed loops, or panel limits reject the edit and keep the last valid model. **Reset to assembly defaults** restores its source dimensions.
- **Export assembly**, under **Manage assembly** in the selected instance’s inspector, saves a `.playwood-assembly.json` file. Its current configurable values, panel color, and finish coverage become the exported design’s defaults; editable controls and attachment corners are preserved. Choose **Library → Assemblies → Import assembly** to add the file to a build. With a selection, import offers the same attachment previews and cancellation as a library module; with no selection, it places a new instance beside the build. Each import creates an independent reusable design in **My assemblies** and supports undo, redo, and autosave.
- Select an instance as a whole from the canvas or the Assembly list. Expand its row to inspect individual panels. **Duplicate** (or **⌘/Ctrl D**) creates a linked copy; inserting from **My assemblies** uses the same definition.
- Choose **Attach a copy**, then hover or focus a **+** in the workspace to preview its placement and resulting hardware. Click the handle to attach. **Attach an existing instance** connects a copy already in the project. **Escape** cancels placement. The complete placement, including connector replacements and any additional matching corners, is one undo step.
- Compatible box corners automatically share cross connectors. Their original 90° hardware is replaced in the assembled creation without changing the reusable box definition. Removing or disconnecting an instance restores the affected corner hardware on its neighbors.
- **Edit shared design** enters an explicit shared editing mode. The breadcrumb identifies the assembly and how many instances will change. Edit its panels or configure the exposed values, then choose **Done editing**. A controlled dimension links to its **Editable values** section, where **Save values** and **Cancel** apply or discard the control draft. Structural edits update every linked instance and keep valid attachments aligned; overrides for retained controls and instance colors are kept. Removing a control restores its dimensions to the shared source. An incompatible edit leaves the last valid construction unchanged.
- Open **Appearance** for separate **Panel color** and **Finish coverage** overrides on the selected instance. Each **Reset** restores only that shared property: resetting coverage keeps the chosen color, and resetting color keeps coverage. **Make independent**, under **Manage assembly**, gives the selected instance its own construction while preserving its panels and physical connections. **Disconnect from neighbors** removes its physical links and places that instance beside the creation; its neighbors stay in place.
- **Disassemble**, under **Manage assembly**, turns the selected instance back into individually editable panels, retaining their positions, finishes, and physical connectors. Other instances stay linked, and the reusable design remains in **My assemblies**. The panels stay selected after disassembly; one Undo restores the instance.

Disassembled panels retain their physical joints. If a panel still connects to a linked instance, use **Detach for free placement** (or **Detach selected panels**) before changing its geometry independently. Detaching restores the affected corner hardware on the remaining instances.

The four-box Diamond arrangement has sixteen panels. Four separate boxes use thirty-two 90° connectors; joining their four shared corners replaces those with sixteen 90° and eight cross connectors. The middle compartment is the space enclosed between the four boxes. The final box can close two matching corners in the same placement.

Instances move as rigid units. Automatic assembly attachment currently handles compatible 90° corner pairs; it does not merge overlapping walls or remove duplicate panels. Definitions, instances, overrides, and physical links are saved with the project and survive undo, reload, JSON export, and import. The parts list counts the resulting physical panels and connectors once.

## Panel cutting SVGs

Choose **Export project → Panel cutting SVGs** to download a separate full-size SVG for each panel with pegboard holes, slots, TROFAST openings, a custom outline, or rounded corners. Each file includes its outer contour and all openings in millimeters, with circular arcs preserved. Panel numbers distinguish repeated names; hidden panels and materialized assembly panels are included. Plain rectangular panels remain in the CSV cutting list.

The SVG uses black outside contours and white inside cuts for tools such as Shaper Origin. Coordinates follow the panel’s top-left corner, independent of its placement in the furniture. Set cut depth and tool offsets on your cutter. TROFAST opening dimensions remain approximations: measure your real insert before cutting.

## Production deployment

The public address is **https://playwood.app/**. Import this repository into Vercel and attach `playwood.app` in the project's domain settings, following the DNS records Vercel provides. `vercel.json` selects Vite, runs `npm run build`, serves `dist`, and sets security headers and immutable caching for hashed assets. The app has one URL (`/`) and needs no server or environment variables.

The HTML includes canonical, Open Graph, and Twitter card metadata. `public/` contains the 1200 × 630 sharing image, favicon, app icons, web manifest, robots rules, and sitemap. The manifest supports standalone launch; offline operation is not implemented. Browser saves retain the original storage key so existing projects survive the branding change.

The header and generated artwork share `public/favicon.svg`. After editing the logo or social card layout, regenerate the committed PNG assets with:

```sh
npx playwright install chromium
node scripts/generate-brand-assets.mjs
```

Asset generation is a development step; Vercel serves the committed files without needing Chromium during the build. Configuration reference: [Vercel project configuration](https://vercel.com/docs/project-configuration/vercel-json).

## Geometry and source references

Manufacturer references:

- [Connector range and current thickness limits](https://www.playwood.it/en/diy-wooden-projects-connectors/)
- [Official metric technical drawings](https://www.playwood.it/site/assets/files/18237/technical_information_playwood_connectors.pdf)
- [Manuals and project inspiration](https://www.playwood.it/en/business-page-landing-post-login/manuals-catalogs/)

Angled connectors accept 16–19 mm panels. T/cross use the current product page's 18–19 mm range; the older technical PDF lists 17–19 mm. The supplied connector models have a 40 mm depth along the joint.

**90° connectors** offer **Plastic** and **Metal** under **Connector material**, both when attaching panels and when editing selected hardware. Metal uses a silver cast finish with procedural grain, surface relief, and soft room reflections. Other connector angles use plastic; changing metal hardware to another type restores plastic. The previous plastic color is retained. Material choices support multiselect, undo/redo, autosave, project export/import, connector filtering, and separate material quantities in the parts dialog and CSV. The metal finish uses the existing 90° CAD profile; it is a visual material variant, not a separate measured metal model.

Connector meshes use the supplied CAD export in `resources/connectors-3d/Connettori sharing.obj`, including the distinct 90°, 105°, 150°, T, and cross profiles and their separate clamp parts. The original OBJ, 3DS, and STEP files are retained in `connectors-3d`; the OBJ is bundled locally, with no external model requests. The geometry is shared across connector instances and used in the studio, attachment previews, and room preview. Panel spacing follows the shoulders in each connector profile, including the continuation branches of T/cross joints. The supplied models depict nominal 18 mm slots and do not include separate screw geometry. Manufacturing tolerances, tightening, material strength, load capacity, and cut-through interference with connectors are not simulated. Front/back attachment previews may require an accessible edge. Starter assemblies are editable design studies, not certified construction plans. Check physical clearances against the official drawings before fabrication.

The app stores panels in local XY coordinates, with thickness on local Z. Project values are millimeters and Euler radians; the renderer converts to meters. Connections form a directed attachment tree, with optional non-driving supports for additional hardware between already positioned panels.

- `src/lib/preview-furnishings.ts`: optional surface recognition and clearance-aware furnishing placement.
- `src/components/PreviewFurnishings.tsx`: procedural books, accessories, decor, seated people, and chairs for the furnished preview.
- `src/components/scene-geometry.ts`: solid/perforated panel geometry.
- `src/components/ui/`: editable shadcn-style Radix primitives; aliases in `components.json`.
- `src/hooks/use-project.ts`: undo/redo and browser-local persistence.
