# playwood. APP

A local, full-window furniture design workspace built with Vite, React, TypeScript, Three.js, React Three Fiber, and shadcn/ui components backed by Radix. Warm neutral colors, restrained controls, and independent scrolling inspectors keep the assembly in view.

For implementation details, see the [architecture and development documentation](docs/README.md). Coding agents should start with [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Run

Requires Node.js 22.12+ (tested with Node 25).

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. No accounts, API keys, backend, or external fonts are required. Projects autosave in this browser's local storage; export a JSON project to keep a portable copy.

## Design workflow

- Start with a shelf, desk, display, a resource example from the Library, or a blank project. **New project** in the header opens a confirmation before clearing the workspace; choosing a full build from the Library or Templates also asks before replacing it. Use **Export current project** there to keep a separate copy, then **Start new project** or **Replace project**. The browser save is replaced too; Undo can restore the previous project until you reload or close the app.
- Add your own panels with dimensions in millimeters, material, finish color, and thickness. Choose solid, pegboard, or rounded slots; holes are real openings through the mesh.
- Under **Material & finish → Material**, choose **OSB** for layered wood strands or **Nazena recycled textile** for a blue-gray compressed-fiber surface, inspired by the [PlayWood kits](https://www.playwood.it/en/shop-kit/). Both have textured faces and cut edges in Studio and Realistic preview. Use **0% Finish coverage** for the natural texture, or increase it for a tinted or solid finish. Material choices support undo/redo, copies, assemblies, autosave, and project exports.
- Panel finish colors come from the project palette. Use **Add color** to create a named swatch, or **Manage palette** to rename, edit, or remove custom colors. The color picker edits a local draft; **Save color** commits it once, then click its swatch to apply it. Editing a palette color updates its uses throughout the project; removing an in-use color lets you choose a replacement. Palette changes support undo/redo and are saved automatically and included in project exports. Older projects' unlisted panel colors become named swatches on opening. New connector joints default to the most-used color among installed connectors, including individual color changes and ignoring removed pieces. This applies to hole filling, automatic connections and splits, move-and-snap, paste attachments, and the attachment form. You can still choose a different color in the form; projects without installed connectors start with red.
- **Finish coverage**, under **Material & finish**, blends from **0%** natural material grain to **100%** solid finish color. Intermediate values show tinted grain. Matching selections can change together; a **Mixed** coverage field is disabled. Coverage changes support undo/redo, autosave, and project files. Assembly instances have their own coverage override under **Appearance**, separate from panel color.
- Open **Shape & surface** in the panel inspector and choose **Create custom outline** to open the vector editor. Drag points, double-click an edge (or use **Add point**) to insert a vertex, or enter exact X/Y coordinates in millimeters from the top left. Grid snapping, Shift to lock a drag axis, Alt to bypass snapping, arrow-key nudging, zoom/pan, and local undo/redo support precise edits. **Apply outline** saves one project edit; **Cancel** discards the draft. Outlines scale with panel width and height, and corner radius rounds both outward and inward corners. The inspector shows the finished shape; **Reset to rectangle** removes the custom outline. Project files retain the normalized vertices and cutting lists export their millimeter coordinates. General connector placement and overlap checks still use the panel’s rectangular bounds; automatically fitted hole closures track their own outline edges. Automatic rectangular splitting is unavailable for custom outlines.
- Select a solid horizontal rectangular panel and open **TROFAST inserts → Add TROFAST item** to fit an IKEA box or compartment tray through a cutout. Choose from eight plastic, mesh, and tray shapes and their available IKEA Switzerland colors. The rim rests on the panel and the body hangs below. The editor checks footprint, a 20 mm border, the floor, surrounding panels (including hidden ones), other inserts, and connector clearance. Rotate the item, click or drag the panel diagram, use its arrow keys (10 mm; Shift: 1 mm), or enter exact center coordinates measured from the panel’s top-left corner. A live 3D preview remains separate until **Add TROFAST box** or **Save TROFAST changes**; Cancel/Escape discards it. Add multiple items where space permits, and edit or remove them from the same section. Items and openings follow their panel, support undo/redo and autosave, and survive project/assembly exports. Product sizes stay fixed when panels resize. The current catalogue is based on IKEA Switzerland; colors and rim/cutout profiles are visual approximations. **Measure the real item before cutting:** the displayed opening and exported cutout notes are not fabrication templates.
- Select a panel in the model or panel list. **Dimensions** and **Connection** or **Placement** come first in the inspector. Expand **Shape & surface** or **Material & finish** for secondary properties; section expansion is remembered for the session. Header badges identify custom shapes and surfaces, fitted TROFAST items, saved constraints (including disabled rules in the current scope), and assembly appearance overrides, even when collapsed. Open **Build tools** operations only when needed. Change dimensions, position, and orientation in their sections. Width and height edits apply on Enter or when leaving the field; connected panels adjust together to keep their joints aligned.
- Open **Snap** in the preview to independently enable the **global grid (G)** and **geometry alignment (S)**. Set grid spacing and snap tolerance in millimeters; preferences are remembered in this browser. The grid starts at the world origin. Geometry alignment uses world-axis panel boundaries (bounding extents for rotated panels), or other connector centers when moving connectors. **Show grid** controls guide visibility separately. Snapping applies to panel drags, resize arrows, and keyboard movement; with the grid enabled, arrows move one grid spacing and Shift moves ten. Joint and hardware limits still apply, and existing drag-to-attach behavior remains available.
- Open **Constraints** in the panel inspector, or use **Add constraint** in the context menu, to keep a measurement as the design changes. Choose **Height above floor** with a top, bottom, or center reference; **Panel dimension**; **Equal panel dimensions**; **Same level above floor**; or **Gap between panels**. Enter millimeters: 80 cm above ground is **800**, and a 20 cm opening is **200**. Height, dimension, and gap rules offer **Exactly**, **At least** (minimum), **At most** (maximum), or **Between** (both limits). For example, a gap can stay between **200** and **300** mm. Values inside the allowed interval remain free; limits do not pull panels back to a boundary. Equal panel dimensions matches local cut width or height; Same level aligns elevation. A gap measures between panel boundaries, including thickness, along X, Y, or Z.
- Constraint changes preview before **Add constraint** or **Save constraint** applies them. For a two-panel rule, **Keep in place for this adjustment** chooses the reference panel; future edits maintain the relationship in either direction. The preview names affected panels and highlights the measurement. **Cancel** or **Escape** discards it. Connected edits preserve joints, connector sizes, and stock limits; a conflicting edit keeps the committed model and explains which rules need attention. With nothing selected, the inspector stays empty; **All constraints** opens the project’s rules explicitly, and **Done** or **Escape** closes that view. Use **Selection** or **All** to review rules, click a row to edit, clear **Enabled** to pause, or remove it. Re-enabling opens a preview. Rules and geometry changes share one Undo step and persist in autosave and project files.
- Click **All constraints** in the preview to configure **Build space**. The **Enable build space** checkbox applies immediately using saved dimensions, with Undo support. Enter exact width, height, depth and X/Z center in millimeters, then **Save build space** to apply dimension changes. The box rests on the floor and must contain your panels. Its outline highlights boundaries within 10 mm; edits beyond the space are rejected. Disable it to pause the limit while retaining dimensions. Settings support Undo, autosave and project export. Hidden and rotated panels count using rectangular stock bounds; hardware and accessories are excluded. The outline is hidden in realistic preview and exploded view.
- Floor, level, and gap rules currently support rectangular panels aligned with world axes; fixed and equal cut dimensions also support rotated/custom-outline panels. Hidden panels still participate. Gap rules measure panel boundaries, not openings, hardware, or clearance from other objects. Author new rules on ordinary panels; use **Editable values** for linked assemblies, or disassemble and disconnect their panels first. Whole-project moves and rotations respect saved rules; a floor reference stays at world Y = 0. Deleting a panel removes its dependent rules with the same undo step. Copies keep eligible internal rules but omit absolute floor requirements and relationships to panels outside the copy. Standalone assembly files do not include project-level constraints.
- Select one panel or connector to show **resize arrows** in the preview. Drag an arrow to pull or push along a permitted direction, making the structure wider, taller, or deeper while keeping connector angles and panel thickness fixed. Panel edge handles keep the opposite edge in place; connected panels adapt together. Hold **Shift before dragging a panel edge arrow** to resize only that panel, keeping other panels and connectors in place. This stops where a joint, connector, or size limit prevents further resizing; arrows perpendicular to the panel cannot resize it independently. The preview shows the change in millimeters and stops at the valid size limit. Release to apply one undoable change, or press **Escape** to cancel. Resize handles are hidden during attachment, exploded view, multi-selection, and linked assembly editing.
- Hold **Control before pulling a panel resize arrow** to repeat its rectangular compartment in that direction. Pull a top panel upward to stack another bay, an outside side panel outward to add a bay beside it, or a front/back edge arrow to grow in depth. The source compartment highlights and the preview snaps to complete bays, showing the repeat count and how many existing panels are reused. Pull back to remove previewed additions; the original construction stays in place. Release applies everything in one Undo step and selects the corresponding panel in the last traversed bay so you can keep extending; **Escape** cancels. With a panel arrow focused, **Control + ArrowUp / ArrowRight / +** adds one bay. Sideways and stacked bays share a boundary panel; depth repeats add a fitted divider between separate four-panel frame sections. If a needed wall, ceiling, or divider already matches the required dimensions, thickness, and placement, it is reused with its existing finish; only missing panels and required joint changes are added. Partly overlapping or differently sized panels still block the repeat. You can pull through complete existing bays to extend farther; releasing over an unchanged bay only advances selection and creates no Undo step. Connector gaps remain part of each bay. New connectors slide along their joints to clear nearby hardware; retained existing connectors stay in place. If there is not enough room, the preview stops before adding that bay. Clearance uses conservative bounds around the CAD parts and still needs checking before fabrication. Repetition uses an empty four-sided compartment with matching 18–19 mm ordinary panels, including rotated frames. For depth repeats, an exactly matching closing panel on the outgoing face can serve as the divider, and you can continue from the newest depth bay with its trailing divider. Other closing faces, internal contents, linked assemblies, custom outlines, and TROFAST panels are excluded. Choose an outside panel when a shared panel borders more than one possible source compartment. Inward normal arrows are unavailable. Existing obstacles, joint fit, saved rules, and project limits can stop growth. Control-drag on the board itself remains **Move & snap**.
- Select a vertical rectangular panel and use **Extend to ground** under **Dimensions** or in its context menu. The lower edge extends exactly to ground level (Y = 0); the upper edge, other panels, and existing connectors stay fixed. Rotated panels can extend along either width or height. The action explains when a joint, collision, assembly constraint, or size limit prevents the extension, and supports undo, redo, and autosave.
- Click a panel in **Studio** and use **arrow keys** to move it **1 cm**, or **Shift + arrow keys** for **10 cm**. Movement follows the current view and the panel’s rotated axes, including after orbiting behind or above the assembly. Connected panels resize while connector angles, thicknesses, and hardware sizes stay fixed. A move that cannot preserve the joints or panel limits leaves the project unchanged and explains why. Free panels and fully selected assemblies translate together. Holding a key forms one undo step. Arrow shortcuts are active only while the canvas has focus; fields and panel-list controls retain their own keys. Finish an attachment or close exploded view before moving panels. In Realistic preview, plain arrow keys drive the room’s vacuum.
- Select at least three parallel panels and choose **Distribute evenly** under **Placement** or in the preview context menu. Panels move along their shared facing direction to leave equal clear gaps, even when tilted or facing opposite ways. The two outermost panels and selected cut sizes stay fixed. Connected spans can resize to retain joints; conflicting rules, insufficient space, or new overlaps reject the edit. Disassemble and disconnect linked assemblies first. Distribution is one undoable edit and persists through autosave and project export.
- Press **X**, **Y**, or **Z** to rotate a selected panel or complete assembly by **90°** around that world axis through the selection’s center. Multiple selected objects turn together. Select all physically connected panels or detach them first; a partial connected selection stays unchanged and explains why. Each press is one undo step, with autosave and redo support. Rotation shortcuts pause during text editing, dialogs, attachment previews, dragging, assembly source editing, and exploded view.
- Press **Shift + X**, **Shift + Y**, or **Shift + Z** to mirror the selection across its center along that world axis (left/right, up/down, or front/back). Select the complete connected structure, as with rotation. Mirrored assembly instances receive a separate mirrored design; other copies stay unchanged. Each press is one undoable edit with autosave and redo. Saved constraints and insert placement rules still apply; an invalid mirror leaves the build unchanged. The same shortcut twice restores the geometry.
- Drag a panel thumbnail to reorder the list. Hover or focus a row for rename, visibility, and remove controls; double-click a name to rename it in place. Amber markers identify overlaps and name the other panels on hover or focus. These changes autosave and support undo.
- Organize the **Panels** and **Connectors** tabs with one level of named groups. Use **New group**, or select rows and choose **Group selected** from the list controls or context menu. Drag a panel thumbnail or connector grip into a group, between groups, or onto **Ungrouped**; dragging a selected row moves the selection together. Use the group checkbox to select or deselect all its items, including while collapsed; a dash indicates a partial selection. Other selections in the same tab are preserved. Groups collapse, accept drops while collapsed, and rename with the pencil, double-click, or **F2**. The group context menu’s **Ungroup** action keeps its contents. Groups and list ordering autosave, export with the project, and support undo/redo.
- Shift-click, Ctrl/⌘-click, or use the panel checkboxes to select multiple panels. Shared parameters stay editable and apply to the entire selection; differing values show **Mixed** and are disabled. Related edits preserve mixed finishes and perforation spacing. Select all with **⌘/Ctrl A**.
- Press **B**, or choose **Box select** beside Pan view, then drag a rectangle around parts. Candidate parts highlight while drawing; release to select panels and connectors together and return to normal navigation. Hold **Shift** while drawing to add both kinds to the existing selection, or press **Escape** to cancel without changing it. Parts must be fully enclosed; the box reaches through overlapping parts and skips hidden parts. With a mixed selection, the inspector follows the active **Panels** or **Connectors** tab under Assembly. Selecting only one kind opens its inspector. Linked assemblies select as whole instances; while editing an assembly, the box selects only its own parts.
- Select one panel and press **A**, choose **Attach a panel (A)** in the preview context menu, or click **Attach a panel** in the inspector to connect a new or existing panel to any of six sides. Match the **A–F labels** in the inspector and preview, hover or focus either control to highlight its side, or click a preview label to select it. Click the selected side again, in either place, to toggle **Flip joint direction**. Choosing a different side keeps the current flip setting. Side names stay attached to the panel as you orbit, and leader lines keep opposite sides identifiable in straight-on views. Explore 90°, 105°, 150°, T, and cross joints, offsets, insets, flip direction, connector color, and quantity. Ghost previews update as settings change.
- T and cross joints add the complete set of branches. Changes to a parent propagate to its connected descendants. Detaching preserves world positions.
- Select connectors in the preview or **Connectors** list, then choose **90°**, **105°**, or **150°** under **Connector type** to change their joint angles. Every clamp on each affected seam changes together, including unselected clamps. The attached panels and their connected branches follow the new angle: changing a back panel’s bottom joint from 90° to 150° tilts the back and its attached top backward by 60°, keeping the base fixed. Conflicting supports, new panel overlaps, saved constraints, or build-space limits leave the construction unchanged with an explanation. Disassemble affected linked assemblies before changing their internal angles; occupied T/cross branches must be detached before replacing those joints with angled connectors. Choosing **T** or **Cross** still changes only selected hardware to offer extra openings. Angle edits support one-step Undo, autosave, and project export/reload.
- **Auto-add connectors**, under **Build tools** or in the toolbar, also upgrades existing corner or T joints into complete T or cross joints. Select the two panels to join; an unselected upright at the same seam can complete the joint. Clamps fit the straight overlap shared by every panel, including different panel sizes and offsets, and find room away from existing corner hardware. Matching panels keep their positions. Loose panels within 100 mm of a compatible joint automatically move to the nearest valid fit without resizing or rotating. With one panel selected, only that panel moves; connected panels stay fixed. This also works at the middle of an upright or shelf, where a T joint needs a split. If an edge needs a small size correction (up to 50 mm), **Resize panel to connect?** shows the proposed width or height before anything changes. **Resize and connect** adjusts that edge and adds the connectors together; **Cancel** keeps the project unchanged. If a gap between connected angled panels needs both lengths to change, **Resize panels to connect?** lists both proposed dimensions before applying them. Each length may change by up to 100 mm; surrounding panels, installed connectors, angles, and thicknesses stay fixed. Selecting one branch can find its nearby partner, including an unselected panel. Cancel or Escape discards the proposal; accepting resizes both and fits the joint in one Undo step. Incompatible angles, blocked hardware, collisions, saved rules, and linked assemblies can prevent a fit. If an upgrade changes a reusable assembly's own hardware, that instance becomes independent; its source and other copies stay unchanged. The result reports upgraded joints and affected instances, and the entire operation supports Undo.
- **Connect with a panel** reverses this workflow: select two or more connectors in the preview or connector list, then use the inspector or context menu. Every piece needs a compatible free T/cross opening (for example, change selected 90° hardware to T first). The panel fits the actual connector positions, including slides and rotated designs. Opposing openings set its span; an unbounded end extends 360 mm, and the other edges cover the full 40 mm grips. Misalignment, occupied slots, incompatible stock, panel collisions, and obstructing hardware prevent the edit. Existing panels and hardware stay in place; affected assembly instances become independent. Supports undo, redo, autosave, and JSON round trips.
- Select two matching parallel rectangular panels and open **Build tools → Divide evenly** to choose the number of dividers. Only counts that fit the current joints and available space are offered; each divider adds one equal section. **Split in the middle** is the one-divider shortcut. Select all four matching sides of an opening for **Divide into a grid**, then set **X dividers** for columns and **Y dividers** for rows: one on each axis creates a 2 × 2 grid. Either axis can be zero, with at least one divider overall. Splitting preserves the outside dimensions, includes T and cross hardware as needed, and updates the cutting list in one undoable edit. It requires 18–19 mm rectangular stock and panels outside linked assemblies.
- The Everyday shelf uses eight 90° connectors at its corners and four T connectors at the middle shelf. Each side has separate lower and upper panels with the 35 mm gap required by the T body. Recognized copies of the former five-panel template are repaired on load/import while preserving finishes, names, shelf positions, and overall dimensions; the load repair supports Undo.
- Thickness, shared-edge, and embedded edge-joint errors block new attachments. Panel overlaps, inaccessible face clamp positions, and supports that have moved out of alignment appear as review notes. The inspector and parts list identify the actual connector types.
- Use **Rescale creation** above the canvas to change repeated measurements throughout the project. Matching widths and heights share one control; thickness, rounded corners, and active hole sizes and spacing have their own controls. The dialog shows use counts and allowed ranges, including connector-compatible thicknesses. Connected spans and fitted inserts adapt together while hardware stays its original size. All changes apply at once, preserve reusable assembly sources and instance controls, and support undo/redo and autosave. Conflicting values leave the project unchanged with an explanation.
- In **Rescale creation**, enable **Only nice values** and choose a millimeter step (presets include **5 mm** and **10 mm**, or enter a custom step from **1–100 mm**). **Apply rescale** rounds existing panel widths, heights, radii, and active perforations, including measurements used only once. With 10 mm steps, 112 mm becomes 110 mm and 118 mm becomes 120 mm. Later length fields round on Enter or blur; resize arrows and free panel drags round on release so the preview stays smooth. Keyboard arrows use the chosen step (Shift: ten steps). Stock thickness, hardware sizes, angles, and counts remain exact. Connected geometry and assembly relationships can require exact dependent measurements; incompatible rounding is rejected. The option and initial rescale share one Undo step and persist in project files and autosave. Clear the option and apply to return to unrestricted input.
- Use **Move creation** above the canvas to translate every panel and connector together, including hidden panels and separate pieces. Enter X, Y, and Z offsets in millimeters, or use **Center horizontally**, **Near back wall**, and **Place on floor** to set them. Negative X moves left; negative Z moves toward the back wall. The wall preset leaves 50 mm clearance. Click **Move creation** to apply the offsets without changing sizes, relative positions, or connections; Undo restores the previous placement.
- Use **Rotate creation** above the canvas to rotate the whole assembly by ±90°, ±45°, or ±180°. Choose Z to tilt sideways into a diagonal layout, X to tip forward/backward, or Y to turn left/right. Every panel (including hidden panels and separate pieces) and connector moves together around the assembly center; its lowest panel height stays unchanged. Rotation autosaves, supports undo/redo, and is preserved in exported projects and the room preview.
- Orbit, pan, zoom, inspect orthographic views, show dimensions, and explore the exploded view. Export editable JSON or a CSV cutting/parts list.
- Choose **Realistic preview** to see your assembled furniture at its actual scale in a modern room with daylight, oak flooring, and familiar furnishings. The 165 cm floor lamp provides a size reference; dimensions describe the visible assembly. Orbit and zoom to explore, press **F** to reset the room view, or return to **Studio** (or press **Escape**) for the full inspector with your selection and view settings preserved. The room renders locally without external assets or API keys.
- A black **Dreame X50 Ultra Complete** vacuum waits at its base station for about ten seconds, then slowly cleans the room around the furniture and your visible construction. With the room canvas focused, hold **↑ / ↓** to drive forward or reverse and **← / →** to turn; it resumes cleaning from its current position after ten seconds without driving input. Collision clearance still applies. It pauses while the browser tab is hidden. Returning to the room or editing your construction starts a fresh route from the dock. Toggling **Auto furnish** updates its route while preserving its position and the remaining wait. The vacuum and dock are preview scenery and do not change your project, parts list, or exports; their appearance and obstacle clearance are visual approximations.
- Enable **Auto furnish** in the room card to add books and decor to shelves, a lamp and computer with keyboard and mouse to suitable tables, and seated people on benches or chairs at desks. It starts off; surface detection and extra rendering run only while enabled in Realistic preview. Placement uses level surfaces, dimensions, support, and available clearance, so ambiguous, tilted, or custom-outline surfaces may stay empty. Furnishings adapt to visible geometry, use the room's lighting, and never become project parts or change exports. The choice lasts for the current session; disable it to remove the added objects and reduce rendering work.
- Shelf compartments mix books, plants or vases, and intentionally empty space. Exposed cabinet tops stay clear or receive occasional decor. Books vary in count, color, thickness, height, and cover design, with standing rows supported at a side wall and leaning books at the free end. Placement uses each row's actual width and stays on the shelf even where connectors leave a gap to the wall. Boards without side support and short compartments can receive flat stacks. Arrangements stay stable while navigating the preview, and spines face the opening inferred from side and back panels.
- Bench detection also recognizes seats extending from shelving, with a leg at the free end and support through the shelving at the attached end. It uses the seat's long axis and tries clear positions along both long edges, so a shelf above one end does not exclude an exposed seating area. Seated figures need room for their body, head, and feet; narrow ledges, unsupported extensions, and fully obstructed seats stay unoccupied.

Keyboard: **A** opens the new-panel attachment preview for one selected panel; **B then drag** selects a rectangular area (**Shift** while drawing adds); **Arrows** move the Studio selection 1 cm; **Shift + arrows** move it 10 cm; plain **Arrows** drive the vacuum in Realistic preview; **X / Y / Z** rotate the selection 90°; **F** fits the view; **⌘/Ctrl Z** undoes; **⌘/Ctrl Shift Z** redoes; **⌘/Ctrl D** duplicates; **Delete** removes a panel; **Escape** cancels selection/preview. Text fields retain normal editing shortcuts. The keyboard button beside the view controls opens the navigation and selection shortcut reference.

In the panel and connector lists, **F2** edits a focused panel or group name. Focus a thumbnail or connector grip and press **Space** to pick it up, **↑/↓** (or **Home/End**) to choose its position or group, then **Space/Enter** to drop or **Escape** to cancel. Renaming accepts **Enter** to save and **Escape** to cancel.

## Illustrated building guide

Choose **Guide** in the header (the book icon on small screens), or **Shortcuts & help** in the status bar, to open the field guide. Its 34 searchable techniques cover starting and replacing projects, panels, finishes and palettes, attachment and connector types, automatic joints, closing openings, equal and grid splitting, connected and independent panel resizing, movement and snapping, user constraints for dimensions and spacing, moving and rescaling the whole creation, rotation, copy/paste, box selection and groups, reusable assemblies and their editable controls, room scale and automatic furnishing, review, and project and assembly files.

Every technique has four steps with an illustrated example, exact controls, an explanation, the expected result, and relevant tips. **Resize connected furniture** includes Control-pull repetition alongside connected and independent resizing. Start with the suggested setup, move through **Next step**, or jump to a numbered step. Related guides connect techniques into longer workflows. The guide remembers your reading position for the current browser session; browsing it never changes the project. **Keyboard shortcuts** remains available as a separate quick reference.

Illustrations project real example panel geometry and connector models into SVG. Only the current step is drawn; help loads on demand and adds no WebGL viewport or continuous rendering loop. Smaller screens use a collapsible technique browser and a stacked lesson layout.

## Reusable assemblies

Build a box once and reuse its construction as linked instances. Select the box's panels, open **Build tools → Create assembly**, enter an **Assembly name**, and choose **Create assembly**. The selected panels become its first instance, and the reusable design appears under **My assemblies** in the Library. The **Cube** library example is a four-panel starting point.

Select an assembly or the panels around a rectangular or polygonal opening, then open **Close holes** under **Build tools** in the inspector. One opening offers a direct action; multiple openings offer a choice such as Front or Back, with panel dimensions. Compartments are detected separately, including on rotated designs. Angled frames and rectangular frames with a side split into several boards produce a **Custom outline** panel; its listed width and height describe the bounding cut size. Closing fits a solid panel and connectors around the perimeter, accounting for the connector seats. A split side gets supports on each of its boards. Simple four-board rectangular joints can upgrade to T or cross connectors, and neighboring outline fills can share a T connector. New connectors slide along the edges to clear existing hardware and each other, using the supplied CAD shapes. A short edge can use one clamp instead of two. Retained frame hardware stays in place; shared joints replace their superseded clamps. An opening that cannot accept compatible hardware is left unchanged. For assemblies, the selected instance receives an independent design containing the new panel; other copies retain their openings. Resizing the surrounding frame reshapes an attached outline panel to keep its edges fitted, including compatible assembly size changes. Detach the closing panel before changing its outline independently. Closing and reshaping support undo, redo, autosave, and export.

When ordinary frame panels have different depths or offsets, a flat filler may need their front or back edges aligned first. Choices marked **Align edges** list each required dimension change before **Align edges & close hole** applies it. The tool extends edges by at most 100 mm to one plane, keeps opposite edges fixed, and preserves existing frame hardware. Neighboring outline fillers on that face move together with their supporting edges, so they can be closed in either order. Selecting or dismissing a choice changes nothing; alignment and filling form one Undo step. The context menu opens this review instead of applying adjustments immediately. Obstacles, saved constraints, linked assemblies, or an incompatible existing joint can prevent an alignment proposal.

- Open **Editable values** when creating an assembly, or choose **Edit values** in **Edit shared design**, to add named controls. Choose the panel dimensions each value controls; one value can drive several panels and dimensions. For example, bind both dimensions of every cube panel to **Side length**. The first dimension sets the default, and further dimensions retain any size difference as an offset. **Relationships** lets you change each binding's multiplier and offset. Use **Save values** to apply shared control edits.
- The **Cube module** comes with one **Side length** control. **Wide shelf module**, tall boxes, and shallow boxes expose **Width**, **Height**, and **Depth** separately. Cubbies expose each column's width and each row's height, plus shared depth; columns run left to right and rows bottom to top. These values describe panel cut lengths in millimeters. Connector clearances and panel thickness add to the final outside dimensions.
- Configure a selected instance under **Dimensions** in its inspector. Its dimensions change without changing the source defaults or other instances' configured values. Connected panels and connectors adjust together, and linked neighbors move as needed to keep shared corners aligned. Incompatible shared depths, closed loops, or panel limits reject the edit and keep the last valid model. **Reset to assembly defaults** restores its source dimensions.
- **Export assembly**, under **Manage assembly** in the selected instance’s inspector, saves a `.playwood-assembly.json` file. Its current configurable values, panel color, and finish coverage become the exported design’s defaults; editable controls and attachment corners are preserved. Choose **Library → Assemblies → Import assembly** to add the file to a build. With a selection, import offers the same attachment previews and cancellation as a library module; with no selection, it places a new instance beside the build. Each import creates an independent reusable design in **My assemblies** and supports undo, redo, and autosave.
- Select an instance as a whole from the canvas or the Assembly list. Expand its row to inspect individual panels. **Duplicate** (or **⌘/Ctrl D**) creates a linked copy; inserting from **My assemblies** uses the same definition.
- Choose **Attach a copy**, then hover or focus a **+** in the workspace to preview its placement and resulting hardware. Click the handle to attach. **Attach an existing instance** connects a copy already in the project. **Escape** cancels placement. The complete placement, including connector replacements and any additional matching corners, is one undo step.
- Compatible box corners automatically share cross connectors. Their original 90° hardware is replaced in the assembled creation without changing the reusable box definition. Removing or disconnecting an instance restores the affected corner hardware on its neighbors.
- **Edit shared design** enters an explicit shared editing mode. The breadcrumb identifies the assembly and how many instances will change. Edit its panels or configure the exposed values, then choose **Done editing**. A controlled dimension links to its **Editable values** section, where **Save values** and **Cancel** apply or discard the control draft. Structural edits update every linked instance and keep valid attachments aligned; overrides for retained controls and instance colors are kept. Removing a control restores its dimensions to the shared source. An incompatible edit leaves the last valid construction unchanged.
- Open **Appearance** for separate **Panel color** and **Finish coverage** overrides on the selected instance. Each **Reset** restores only that shared property: resetting coverage keeps the chosen color, and resetting color keeps coverage. **Make independent**, under **Manage assembly**, gives the selected instance its own construction while preserving its panels and physical connections. **Disconnect from neighbors** removes its physical links and places that instance beside the creation; its neighbors stay in place.
- **Disassemble**, under **Manage assembly**, turns the selected instance back into individually editable panels, retaining their positions, finishes, and physical connectors. Other instances stay linked, and the reusable design remains in **My assemblies**. The panels stay selected after disassembly; one Undo restores the instance.

Disassembled panels retain their physical joints. If a panel still connects to a linked instance, use **Detach for free placement** (or **Detach selected panels**) before changing its geometry independently. Detaching restores the affected corner hardware on the remaining instances.

The four-box Diamond arrangement has sixteen panels. Four separate boxes use thirty-two 90° connectors; joining their four shared corners replaces those with sixteen 90° and eight cross connectors. The middle compartment is the space enclosed between the four boxes. The final box can close two matching corners in the same placement.

Instances move as rigid units. Automatic assembly attachment handles compatible 90° corner pairs and offers shared-panel placements when complete walls coincide. Shared-panel placement merges duplicate panels and refits overlapping hardware; affected instances become ordinary panels, as indicated in the placement preview. Definitions, instances, overrides, and physical links are saved with the project and survive undo, reload, JSON export, and import. The parts list counts the resulting physical panels and connectors once.

Select panels, connectors, or a mixture and press **⌘/Ctrl C** (or choose **Copy** in the context menu). Connector selections include the panels joined by that hardware. Copies retain the selected panels’ relative placement, complete internal joints, and hardware overrides; external joints stay behind. Select a destination panel or assembly and press **⌘/Ctrl V**, preview a **+**, then click to attach the whole copy in one undo step. **Escape** cancels. A single whole assembly stays linked; other multi-panel selections paste as ordinary panels. The clipboard lasts for the current session.

## Geometry and source references

Manufacturer references:

- [Connector range and current thickness limits](https://www.playwood.it/en/diy-wooden-projects-connectors/)
- [Official metric technical drawings](https://www.playwood.it/site/assets/files/18237/technical_information_playwood_connectors.pdf)
- [Manuals and project inspiration](https://www.playwood.it/en/business-page-landing-post-login/manuals-catalogs/)

Angled connectors accept 16–19 mm panels. T/cross use the current product page's 18–19 mm range; the older technical PDF lists 17–19 mm. The supplied connector models have a 40 mm depth along the joint.

**90° connectors** offer **Plastic** and **Metal** under **Connector material**, both when attaching panels and when editing selected hardware. Metal uses a silver cast finish with procedural grain, surface relief, and soft room reflections. Other connector angles use plastic; changing metal hardware to another type restores plastic. The previous plastic color is retained. Material choices support multiselect, undo/redo, autosave, project export/import, connector filtering, and separate material quantities in the parts dialog and CSV. The metal finish uses the existing 90° CAD profile; it is a visual material variant, not a separate measured metal model.

Connector meshes use the supplied CAD export in `resources/connectors-3d/Connettori sharing.obj`, including the distinct 90°, 105°, 150°, T, and cross profiles and their separate clamp parts. The original OBJ, 3DS, and STEP files are retained in `connectors-3d`; the OBJ is bundled locally, with no external model requests. The geometry is shared across connector instances and used in the studio, attachment previews, and room preview. Panel spacing follows the shoulders in each connector profile, including the continuation branches of T/cross joints. The supplied models depict nominal 18 mm slots and do not include separate screw geometry. Manufacturing tolerances, tightening, material strength, load capacity, and cut-through interference with connectors are not simulated. Front/back attachment previews may require an accessible edge. Starter assemblies are editable design studies, not certified construction plans. Check physical clearances against the official drawings before fabrication.

The app stores panels in local XY coordinates, with thickness on local Z. Project values are millimeters and Euler radians; the renderer converts to meters. Connections form a directed attachment tree, with optional non-driving supports for additional hardware between already positioned panels.
